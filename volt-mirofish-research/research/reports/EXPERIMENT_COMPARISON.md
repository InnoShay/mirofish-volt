## 2. CROSS-EXPERIMENT THEME EVOLUTION

### 1. From "Curiosity" (Exp A) to "Vulnerability Analysis" (Exp B)
- In Experiment A, agents reacted with curiosity to Volt’s claim of `<50ms` logging and replacing timesheets. Agent 25 (Simon Kestler) planned a video, and Agent 3 (Elena Rostova) was eager to test the CLI.
- In Experiment B, the tone turned acutely analytical. Agent 4 (Martin Lindqvist) introduced Goodhart’s Law, prompting Agent 20 (Nadia Petrov) to analyze LLM length and vocabulary bias in commit summaries. Agent 21 (Felix Baumgartner) created an emergent post on "B2B SaaS Fatigue", which received significant community upvotes (25 likes).

### 2. From "Ideological Resistance" (Exp B) to "Operational Problem Solving" (Exp C)
- While Experiment B concluded that automated algorithmic compensation is dangerously prone to gaming and cultural damage, Experiment C confronted the reality of an actual 14-person startup seeking to eliminate timesheet dread.
- Instead of outright rejection, Experiment C produced actionable conditions for adoption:
  - *Agent 9 (VP Eng):* "To trust this, you need transparent, immutable audit trails and a senior-level override."
  - *Agent 17 (Senior Backend):* "The key is absolute transparency in the scoring algorithm. The rubric must be auditable, not just a black-box AI output."
  - *Agent 8 (Head of Ops):* "Implementation must ensure total transparency, auditability, and clear alignment with established labor policies."

## 3. UNEXPECTED CROSS-EXPERIMENT DISCOVERIES
1. **The Emergent Peer-Upvoting Phenomenon:** In Experiment B, when Agent 4 and Agent 19 posted critical analyses warning of "Digital Taylorism", agents did not merely comment—they engaged in heavy upvoting (`like_post: 25`). Skeptical theses acted as focal points for collective agreement.
2. **The Desire for "Negative Space" Scoring:** Across all three experiments, the most passionate developer defense was not for higher feature points, but for **protecting refactoring, code deletion, and mentorship**.
3. **The Power of the Co-Author Handshake:** Whenever mentioned, Volt’s co-author duplicate credit rule ($N \le 3$) neutralized accusations of selfish individual competition, proving to be one of Volt's most defensible features.
