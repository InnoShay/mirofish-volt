### 1. The Goodhart Trap & The "Prompt Engineering of Commits"
- **Vulnerability:** When points translate directly to financial payout, human ingenuity invariably shifts from solving business problems to optimizing the scoring mechanism.
- **The Eloquent Summarizer Problem:** An engineer with strong verbal skills might write a beautifully crafted commit summary framing a routine refactor as an "architectural paradigm shift ($C=8$)", receiving higher AI scores than an engineer who shipped a complex concurrency fix with a terse one-line summary.
- **Defense Mechanism in Volt:** The Evidence Multiplier ($E$) requires verified artifacts (GitHub PRs, test suites, benchmarks), and the manager retains direct review and override authority.

### 2. The Cooperative Cannibalism Risk (Zero-Sum Pool)
- **Vulnerability:** In fixed bonus pools, one contributor gaining points reduces everyone else’s share. Teammates may become reluctant to help others or review colleagues' pull requests promptly, fearing they are diluting their own payout.
- **Counter-Dynamic in Volt:** Volt’s Co-Author Handshake provides full credit duplication for up to 3 collaborators, explicitly incentivizing pairing and unblocking teammates.

### 3. The Subjectivity of Cognitive Complexity
- **Vulnerability:** Who objectively defines whether a distributed consensus fix is a $C=5$, $C=8$, or $C=13$? Cognitive complexity is inherently subjective across domains (frontend CSS vs. kernel optimization vs. statistical modeling).
- **Misunderstanding:** Users may assume the AI uses a mysterious proprietary black-box formula, whereas Volt exposes the explicit mathematical power table ($C^{1.4}$) and allows custom written plain-language rubrics.

### 4. Overfitting to Software Engineering
- **Vulnerability:** The Git terminology (`add`, `commit`, `staging`, `PR links`) creates an immediate mental affinity for coders, but introduces significant friction for marketing, legal, sales, and operations teams who do not understand Git paradigms.

### 5. Privacy & Corporate Security Resistance
- **Vulnerability:** Enterprise security teams often forbid third-party AI agents from reading commit messages, code diffs, or internal technical deliverables due to confidentiality, IP leakage, and regulatory compliance (SOC2, GDPR).
