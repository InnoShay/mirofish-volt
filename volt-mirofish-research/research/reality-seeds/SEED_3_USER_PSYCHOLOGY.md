# REALITY SEED 3: USER PSYCHOLOGY
**Domain:** Professional Motivations, Decision-Making & Cognitive Biases  
**Integrity Level:** Verified Behavioral Patterns in Tech & Knowledge Organizations  

---

### 1. Developer & Technical Practitioner Psychology
- **Autonomy & Craft Pride:** Software engineers and technical researchers value agency above almost all else. Surveillance software or perceived micromanagement triggers fierce resistance.
- **Workflow Interruption Aversion:** Engineers experience severe context switching penalties when forced out of flow to update external management tools. Tools must live inside their existing environment (terminal, shell, IDE).
- **Fear of Algorithmic Incompetence:** Developers are deeply aware of edge cases and hallucinations in LLMs. The prospect of an "AI Judge" evaluating their code complexity evokes dread of being misjudged by an algorithm that misses subtle domain difficulty.
- **Fairness & Transparency:** If a grading system exists, engineers demand full visibility into the source code, weights, formulas, and reasoning. Hidden multipliers or proprietary heuristics breed cynicism.

### 2. Founder & Executive Psychology
- **Need for Truth over Theater:** Founders are exhausted by status reports and polished sprint demos that obscure critical delivery bottlenecks. They crave objective proof of unblocked dependencies.
- **Budget Predictability vs. Performance Leverage:** Founders want to incentivize exceptional output with upside bonuses, but require safeguards against budget runaway and metric gaming.
- **Time Scarcity:** Founders do not want to spend hours reviewing timesheets; they need automated summaries with clear exception handling.

### 3. Engineering Manager & Operations Psychology
- **Accountability Shield:** Middle managers are caught between executive demands for velocity and developer demands for autonomy. A neutral, evidence-based ledger provides an objective foundation for 1-on-1s.
- **Fear of Interpersonal Conflict:** Transparent point systems and bonus splits can ignite peer rivalry, envy, or accusations of favoritism if rubrics are ambiguous.

### 4. Junior vs. Senior Dynamics
- **Senior Engineers:** Suffer most under hourly timesheets (their brevity looks like less work). They stand to gain substantially from complexity and impact weightings.
- **Junior & Mid-Level Engineers:** May feel intimidated by steep non-linear power curves ($C^{1.4}$) if routine bug fixes and maintenance tasks receive low base scores, creating anxiety around career progression and fair bonus shares.
